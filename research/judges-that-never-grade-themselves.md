# Build judges that never grade themselves

The literature supports nearly every earlier proposal for the Jev fleet. It also exposes a flaw those proposals share: in several places a component's own output is allowed to count as evidence about that same component. All four problems point to the same remedy. Signals a system produces about itself are flat or harmful. When GPT-3.5 corrected itself with no outside input, its CommonsenseQA accuracy fell from **75.8% to 38.1%**. When two LLMs are both wrong, they pick the same wrong answer **about 60%** of the time, against 33% by chance. Model updates across nine commercial APIs fixed failures that every model shared at about a third of the expected rate. Evaluations built only on outcomes the system chose to observe "heavily underestimate failure rates." This report's single design principle follows: **no component may be graded on evidence it produced or selected**. That covers the agent, the Jev, the gates, the humans and the dashboards. The log must therefore record, for every label, *how it was selected and with what probability*. In practice this takes the design past the earlier proposals in five ways:

- The agent harness needs **norms and consolidation** in addition to continuity, correction and commitment.
- The Jev's −1/0/+1 distribution is a *first-order* score. It should sit inside conformal, selective-risk gates defined over overlapping regions that do not depend on the judge.
- Abstention should be priced as error cost plus escalation cost minus value of information. Routing should use the difference between model and human loss, not model confidence.
- Independence between judges and labellers has to be *measured*, not assumed.
- Every party, humans included, should earn a proper-scoring track record from blind audit items drawn at random.

Build the log schema first, then the committed-seed audit stream, then the gates. Everything else depends on those three.

## The imaginary agent needs norms and consolidation, not just a loop

Agency, as both philosophy and industry define it, is a closed loop with the environment. The enactivist definition adds conditions that "continuity, correction, commitment" leaves out. Barandiaran, Di Paolo and Rohde require **individuality, interactional asymmetry and normativity**, meaning the agent regulates its activity *according to norms* ([PhilArchive](https://philarchive.org/rec/BARDAI-6)). Anthropic's working definition is "LLMs using tools based on environmental feedback in a loop", where agents "gain 'ground truth' from the environment at each step" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)). The earlier three constraints cover the loop and part of individuality. They do not say what counts as success. A hear–remember–make agent therefore needs an explicit, re-read statement of what it is for, against which the world's replies are judged. Without one, "correction" has no direction.

### What the evidence confirms

**Correction is load-bearing, and only when the signal comes from outside.** Removing self-verification against game state cut Voyager's discovered items by **73%**, the largest drop of any feedback type. A random curriculum cut them by **93%** ([Voyager](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.16291)). Self-correction with no outside signal ranged from flat to harmful. GPT-4 on HotpotQA fell from 49.0 to 43.0. When GPT-3.5 changed an answer, it more often went from correct to incorrect than the reverse. The same procedure looks helpful with oracle labels (CommonsenseQA 89.7) because the gain is the external signal ([Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01798v2)). Multi-agent debate scored 83.2 on GSM8K against 85.3 for self-consistency at equal compute, so adding internal voices does not substitute for the world ([Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01798v2)).

**Commitment (append, never erase) has a mechanistic justification beyond accountability.** Models trained recursively on their own outputs lose the tails of the distribution first and then collapse ([Shumailov et al., *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y)). *Accumulating* synthetic data alongside real data avoids collapse across language models, diffusion models and VAEs. In the linear case, test error has an upper bound that does not grow with the number of iterations ([Gerstgrasser et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01413)). Carrying this to agent memory is an inference, not a tested agent result. An agent that overwrites its notes with its own rewrites is in the "replace" regime and drifts. An agent that keeps verbatim human messages and world responses as a permanent ground layer is in the "accumulate" regime. MemGPT never deletes evicted messages ([MemGPT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.08560)). mini-SWE-agent's linear history, where "the trajectory is the same as the messages sent to the LM", is described as an asset for debugging and fine-tuning ([mini-SWE-agent](https://github.com/SWE-agent/mini-swe-agent)).

### What the evidence qualifies

**Continuity helps only when memory is processed.** In Generative Agents, the full architecture scored a TrueSkill μ of **29.89**. Removing reflection dropped it to 26.88. The memory-stream-only condition scored **21.21**, below human crowdworkers at 22.95 ([Park et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03442)). A raw log was the worst condition tested. MemGPT's tiered memory raised GPT-4's deep-retrieval accuracy from **32.1% to 92.5%** ([MemGPT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.08560)). Workflows induced from past trajectories added **24.6% and 51.1%** relative success on Mind2Web and WebArena ([Agent Workflow Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07429)). Every successful system pairs an append-only raw layer with a derived layer that consolidates it: recall plus working context, memory stream plus reflections, trajectories plus a skill library. The good regulator theorem says the same in principle: a regulator must hold a *model* of what it regulates, not a history ([Good regulator theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_regulator_theorem)). Commitment needs a partner, **consolidation**: summarize upward and never delete downward.

**"No code execution" removes the channel that every high-scoring harness keeps.** mini-SWE-agent is about 100 lines with bash as its only tool and scores **above 74% on SWE-bench Verified** ([mini-SWE-agent](https://github.com/SWE-agent/mini-swe-agent)). Its ground truth arrives in milliseconds through execution. Reflexion's coding gains (91% vs 80% pass@1) come from *executing* tests the model wrote for itself ([Reflexion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366)). A write-only agent regulates the world only through text that others act on, so the humans on the hearing channel become its actuators and its sensors. The design still works. Its correction loop simply runs at human latency, and it is limited to domains where humans or other systems are the environment.

### What it suggests building

Five mechanisms follow.

**Two-stratum repo.** The window compiler becomes a projection builder over an immutable event log. It always includes a verbatim slice of external inputs alongside the agent's own summaries. Shumailov's finding that keeping 10% original data per generation causes only minor degradation is a sensible starting ratio ([Shumailov et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y)).

**Consolidation commits while idle.** Offline "sleep-time" thinking over stored context needed about **5× less test-time compute** for equal accuracy and cut amortized cost per query by 2.5× ([Lin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13171)). A write-only agent can do this natively by committing revised `MODEL.md`-style projections between messages.

**A charter file.** This supplies Barandiaran's normativity. It is re-read on every window build and versioned like everything else.

**The Jev as the agent's substitute for execution.** A 7 ms judge that scores drafts gives the zero-code agent per-step feedback faster than any human can. It counts as *external* correction only to the extent the Jev is calibrated against the world (section 2) and comes from a different lineage than the agent. LLM judges systematically inflate scores for models from their own provider ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07962v1)). A Jev distilled from the agent's model family is much closer to self-correction.

**Commitments written as scorable forecasts.** Each "make" registers the question that will resolve it. The hearing channel then closes the loop with an outcome, not just a new request, and every commitment enters the same proper-scoring ledger as the judges (section 5).

Two gaps remain. First, no peer-reviewed study ablates mutable against immutable memory in an LLM agent. Second, none measures whether an agent's identity survives a model swap; the only sources found are practitioner blogs ([ClawSouls](https://blog.clawsouls.ai/posts/cross-model-persona-fidelity/)). Capability clearly does not carry over: GPT-4 found **5.7×** more unique items than GPT-3.5 in Voyager ([Voyager](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.16291)). So identity continuity and competence continuity should be measured separately whenever the model behind a repo changes.

| Correction | Confirmed, strengthened | Must be external and frequent; the Jev can supply it only if independently calibrated |

## The Jev's distribution is a verdict; trust in it must be audited

The Jev outputs a distribution over −1/0/+1. That distribution is *first-order*: it says what the judge believes about the item. The second-order question is how reliable that distribution is in this region of inputs, and the Jev's own output cannot answer it. Bit-identical determinism removes repeat-sampling variance, so only three second-order signals remain: disagreement with other models (seed ensembles), the input's distance from the training data (density), and audited outcomes. **Only the third measures an error rate.**

### What the evidence confirms

**Per-region calibration memory is a recognized formal object**: Mondrian, group-conditional conformal prediction combined with selective-risk control. Geifman and El-Yaniv choose a confidence threshold so that selective risk stays at or below r* with probability 1−δ, using a binomial tail bound on held-out data ([Geifman & El-Yaniv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500)). Applied per cluster, the rule is: keep audited counts (n_k, errors_k), and act only when the Clopper–Pearson upper bound on the confident-error rate is at or below r*. An unaudited cluster has n_k = 0 and a bound of 1, so it never fires. "Untrusted until audited" now carries a formal δ. Audit-sampling tables give the cost. With zero errors observed, **60 items bound the error rate below 4.9% at 95% confidence, and 150 items bound it below 2.0%**. One error in 60 raises the bound to 7.7% ([AICPA tables](https://ezto.mheducation.com/extMedia/bne/Louwers_9e/Module_E/louwers_aicpa_samplesizetables_2.htm)). Calibration memory therefore costs roughly 60–150 labels per region, regardless of traffic.

**Seed ensembles are the strongest practical upgrade.** In Ovadia et al.'s shift benchmark (16 corruptions × 5 intensities across vision, text and click data), ensembles had the best accuracy and lowest calibration error at larger shift. **Most of the gain came from about 5 members** ([Ovadia et al.](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1906.02530)). For the Jev that is roughly 35 ms per item. Determinism also has a real benefit: nonconformity scores are reproducible, so an audit label recorded on one device stays valid calibration data on every device.

### What the evidence overturns or qualifies

**Do not turn the Jev's distribution into a "second-order" Dirichlet head.** It is tempting, because the Jev already emits three numbers. Evidential deep learning's epistemic output is not faithful. Its learned uncertainty can collapse to zero, and its concentration parameters kept growing over 15,000 epochs. In practice the KL regularizer acts as a fixed "uncertainty budget", so the output tracks the regularization weight λ rather than a bootstrap reference ([Jürgens et al., ICML 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.09056v3)). Treat total evidence at most as a novelty feature.

**Dedicated error predictors rarely beat the baseline.** In FD-Shifts, "none of the evaluated methods from literature beats the simple Maximum Softmax Response baseline." Under corruption shift, MC-dropout-averaged softmax scored an AURC of **81.4** against 93.0 for plain softmax and 88.8 for ConfidNet. The Mahalanobis OOD detector did *worse* than softmax at catching misclassifications (16.0 vs 9.77), because it detects unusual inputs, not wrong answers ([FD-Shifts](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.15259)).

**Static thresholds over-trust under drift.** Temperature scaling was well calibrated on i.i.d. data, but its calibration error rose sharply with shift ([Ovadia et al.](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1906.02530)). A gate tuned once on validation data will degrade silently.

**Perturbations are the weakest leg of the earlier proposal.** No source shows test-time augmentation beating seed ensembles at detecting misclassification. Ensembles themselves catch *variance* errors, not *bias* errors. Their diversity is real but bounded ([Fort et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02757)), it can largely be matched by a single larger model ([Abe et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06985)), and most methods stayed confident on fully out-of-distribution inputs ([Ovadia et al.](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1906.02530)).

**Distillation imports overconfidence.** In one teacher–student pair, the student's overconfident ECE was **16.88 against the teacher's 9.27**. Temperature-scaling the teacher before distillation cut the student's figure to 8.49. Teacher calibration explained student accuracy (R² = 0.92) better than teacher accuracy did (0.68) ([teacher calibration in KD](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20224v1)). Students also have low fidelity to their teachers ([Stanton et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05945)), so the teacher's calibration map does not show where the Jev is wrong. Whether ternary weights worsen calibration is unknown; no study was found.

**Calibration and error-ranking are different properties.** In 224,000 LLM trials, Mistral had the best discrimination (d′ 1.597) but the *worst* metacognitive efficiency (M-ratio 0.852). At 50% coverage, Gemma-2 reached 77.4% selective accuracy against Mistral's 70.7%. Temperature moved d′ while meta-d′ stayed stable ([Do LLMs Know What They Know?](https://huggingface.co/papers/2603.25112.md)). A Jev can have low ECE and still rank its own errors badly. Recalibration does not fix that; only a better second-order signal does.

### What it suggests building

**Use conformal sets as the gate's grammar.** Act only when the conformal set is a singleton. A set of {0} is a *verdict*, "neutral." A set of {−1, 0, +1} is "I don't know." Folding both into probability mass on the middle class conflates two different states. That conflation becomes dangerous in section 5, where an "always 0" strategy is the classic uninformative equilibrium.

**Generalize regions from one partition to many overlapping ones.** Multicalibration requires calibration on every set in a class C at once ([Hébert-Johnson et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08513)). Gibbs, Cherian and Candès give exact finite-sample coverage simultaneously over a finite family of groups ([Gibbs et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12616)). The gate rule becomes the *minimum*, over all regions containing x, of each region's lower bound on precision. C should include features independent of the judge, such as source, task type and an embedding from another model. Otherwise a blind spot that cuts across the Jev's own clusters stays invisible.

**Recalibrate online.** Adaptive conformal inference updates α_{t+1} = α_t + γ(α − err_t) and holds long-run coverage under arbitrary shift ([ACI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00170)). Each region's threshold then moves as its audit outcomes move, without retraining. Conformal risk control can bound the false-action rate directly instead of coverage ([Angelopoulos et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02814)).

**Score each item twice.** Combine an ensemble-averaged softmax for in-distribution error ranking with a feature-density score for novelty. Joint scoring is "substantially more reliable" than either alone ([SURE+](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03903)). DDU reaches **97.86 OOD AUROC from one model**, against 97.73 for a 5-ensemble. It requires spectral normalization and residual connections to avoid feature collapse ([DDU](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.11582)). All of these numbers come from vision benchmarks.

**Report two numbers per region**: ECE, and type-2 AUROC/AURC on audited items. Also recalibrate the teacher before any future distillation.

|---|---|---|
| Per-region memory, lower-bound gate | Confirmed | Formalize as Clopper–Pearson selective risk; overlapping regions; min-over-regions; union bound |
| Seed ensembles | Confirmed | ~5 members; catches variance, not shared bias |
| Perturbations | Weakly supported | Lower priority than density scoring |

## Abstention is a purchase whose receipt is a label

The earlier framing is right, and the literature sharpens it. "Abstention death spiral" is not an established term; no source uses it. The phenomenon it names is well studied under the name **selective labels**: outcomes are observed only for the branch the system takes. Lakkaraju et al. found that imputation, including propensity matching, "heavily underestimate[s] failure rates." Their contraction method, which exploits random assignment across decision-makers, had **6.4× smaller error** than the best imputation, and the gap widened as unobserved factors mattered more ([Lakkaraju et al.](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/home/kleinber/kdd17-selective.pdf)). When the system decides which data it collects, a greedy policy converges on its own prior "regardless of the true crime rate" ([Ensign et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v81/ensign18a.html)). The death spiral is that same runaway loop pointed toward caution. The gate's competence estimate for a novel region freezes at a pessimistic prior because abstaining yields no feedback ([Cortes et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03478)). Off-policy evaluation needs every action to have nonzero probability in every context ([Dudík et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.4601)). A deterministic gate that never acts in region R makes the value of acting there *unidentifiable* from its logs.

### What the evidence confirms

Shadow judgments, exploration slots and audit floors are the standard fixes, and each injects randomization or reuses variation that already exists. Logging the Jev's would-be verdict on every escalated item turns escalation from an action that destroys information into one that produces it, at no extra cost. Items that are abstained on and then dropped are the true blind spot. A fixed fraction of them, sampled uniformly within the abstain region with a known propensity, keeps evaluation unbiased. Exploration on *reversible* actions is the safe-exploration form of positivity.

### What the evidence overturns

**Confidence is the wrong abstention signal.** Chow's classical rule rejects when the maximum posterior falls below 1 − c, for a constant c ([Chow 1970](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1970.1054406)). The learning-to-defer literature shows that this rule fails whenever the expert is not an oracle. Under triage, the optimal policy is a per-instance threshold on the **difference between the model's error and the human's error**, and a model trained for full automation "is not necessarily the best one" to use under triage ([Okati et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.08902)). Deferring to a biased expert can make outcomes worse ([Madras et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.06664)). The most accurate model is not the best teammate ([Bansal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13102)). For the Jev, "low confidence → escalate" is wrong in any region where the human or big model is *also* bad. The gate needs a separate error estimate for each escalation target, and only blind audits that score the humans can supply one.

**The price formula.** Combining these results gives an action rule. It is this report's synthesis, not a published result:

**cost(a | x) = E[error cost | a, x] + c_a − VOI_a(x)**

Here a ranges over act, abstain, escalate-to-big-model and escalate-to-human. c_human is the *shadow price* of the attention budget: the Lagrange multiplier that rises as the queue fills. VOI_a(x) is the probability that x's region recurs multiplied by the expected loss reduction per label. A cheap estimate of recurrence is the growth rate of the region's traffic share. VOI is zero for a pure abstain with no audit and positive for shadow-judged escalation and exploration. Seen this way, the death spiral is a pricing error: abstaining looks cheap only because the label it forgoes is priced at zero. No published learning-to-defer paper prices that information value in a non-stationary setting. This is an open niche.

### Human skill atrophy is specific, fast and invisible

Humans keep the *procedural* skills and lose the *cognitive* ones. In a Boeing 747 simulator, pilots largely retained hand-flying. Only **1 of 16** flew an arrival, approach and missed approach without the flight-management computer and without error. When an instrument failed, **75% followed the faulty altimeter** and **94% approached a stall** in the airspeed failure. Mind wandering was 20.0% under autoflight against 6.9% when hand-flying from raw data ([Casner et al.](https://gwern.net/doc/technology/2014-casner.pdf)). Experienced endoscopists averaging 28 years in practice saw their adenoma detection rate fall from **28.4% to 22.4%** within about three months of AI assistance being introduced. The adjusted odds ratio was 0.69, though the study was observational ([ACG summary](https://gi.org/journals-publications/ebgi/zhou_sep2025/); [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2025/08/12/ai-deskilling-doctors-colonoscopy-study-lancet/)). In a 13-person longitudinal sample, GPS use correlated with decline in hippocampal spatial memory at r = −0.68 ([Dahmani & Bohbot](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7156656)). Complacency *grows* with automation reliability and is not prevented by instructions alone ([Parasuraman & Manzey](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720810376055)). This is Bainbridge's irony: the more reliable the system, the less practiced the human who must catch its failures ([Bainbridge](https://doi.org/10.1016/0005-1098(83)90046-8)). For Jev reviewers, the skill that decays is not applying the rubric. It is **forming an independent view before seeing the verdict, and noticing when the instrument is wrong**.

The escalation rate is squeezed from both directions. Clinical studies find **72–99% of alarms are false** ([Sendelbach & Funk](https://doi.org/10.1097/NCI.0b013e3182a903f9)). In one ICU, 88.8% of 12,671 annotated arrhythmia alarms were false positives ([Drew et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0110274)). At that rate humans stop responding. Too few escalations, on the other hand, breed complacency. The answer is a **fixed, deliberately sized human channel**. Audits provide its floor. Real escalations fill the rest by expected loss reduction, up to a cap. **Escalation PPV**, the fraction of escalations where the human changed or confirmed a non-default outcome, should be a first-class metric.

### What it suggests building

**Blind audits pay three times.** Making humans commit before seeing AI output significantly reduced overreliance (N = 199). The most effective designs were also the least liked, and the benefit went mostly to high need-for-cognition users ([Buçinca et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692v1)). Under an hour of probability training with Brier feedback improved forecasters' Brier scores by **6–11%** over controls ([Chang et al.](https://dlab.sauder.ubc.ca/sjdm/journal/16/16511/jdm16511.html)). A blind audit in which the human gives a probability, then sees the resolution and their score, combines both interventions and also produces an unbiased label for the Jev. One unit of attention buys a forcing function, calibration training and ground truth. That is the economic case for an audit floor.

**Target the practice at what decays.** Generic practice did not predict performance on the skills that mattered ([Casner et al.](https://gwern.net/doc/technology/2014-casner.pdf)). Audit sets should oversample likely Jev errors and novel regions, with known propensities. They should also include **seeded fault drills**: items where the Jev is known to be wrong, shown with its verdict. These measure directly whether reviewers still catch a "faulty altimeter."

**Track each reviewer's Brier score over time** as an early warning of deskilling, the equivalent of monitoring adenoma detection rate on unassisted colonoscopies.

**Get contraction for free.** Randomly assigning items to gate thresholds or reviewers creates the variation that lets you estimate a more permissive gate's failure rate without running it unsafely ([Lakkaraju et al.](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/home/kleinber/kdd17-selective.pdf)).

**Close the calibration triangle.** On every audit item:
- model versus world gives the Jev's calibration and off-policy evaluation;
- human versus world gives reviewer Brier scores;
- model versus human gives Okati's loss difference, which drives routing.

|---|---|---|
| Abstention as purchase, VOI × recurrence | Confirmed, formalized | cost = error + c_a − VOI; c_human as shadow price |
| Exploration slots on reversible actions | Confirmed | Required by positivity; log ε as propensity |

## A million identical judges are one judge with one blind spot

With a million bit-identical copies, within-fleet error correlation is exactly 1. Every finding about correlation *across different* models is therefore a lower bound on how badly the fleet's errors compound. Page's diversity identity, collective error = average individual error − prediction diversity, makes this exact: **copies add zero diversity and zero error reduction** ([Diversity prediction theorem](https://RosettaCode.org/wiki/Diversity_prediction_theorem)). They buy throughput, not accuracy.

### What the evidence confirms

**Shared failure is the norm, and upgrades do not reach it.** Across nine commercial APIs, the share of instances that all three vendors in a modality got wrong reached **15–18%** on facial-emotion datasets. These systemic failures are "often roughly half the error rate of the most accurate model." Both all-wrong and all-right outcomes exceeded the independence expectation on *every* dataset. Only about **10% of single-model improvements** landed on instances every other model failed, although those instances made up **27%** of the improvable pool. In one WAIMAI comparison, Amazon fixed 78 systemic failures and created 78 new ones ([Toups et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.05862v1)). This directly supports diffing a new judge **by cluster**. A judge can improve on aggregate while leaving the hard, shared failures exactly as they were. Ship gates should report *churn*, meaning items fixed and items newly broken, not only the net change.

**Averages hide failures that matter.** Hidden stratification produces relative performance gaps above **20%** on clinically important subsets ([Oakden-Rayner et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12475)). Group DRO improves worst-group accuracy by **10–40 points** while keeping average accuracy high ([Sagawa et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08731)), which shows those gaps were there all along and the averages did not reveal them. Per-cluster worst-case reporting is confirmed.

**Baselines drift with the model.** Retraining on data the model itself shaped converges to a *performatively stable* point, a fixed point of the feedback loop rather than the truth ([Perdomo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06673)). Sculley et al. name "hidden feedback loops" and "undeclared consumers" as core ML technical debt ([Sculley et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2015/file/86df7dcfd896fcaf2674f757a2463eba-Paper.pdf)). A dashboard computed on items the Jev let through is censored by the Jev. Hash selection *before* judgment, including suppressed items, is the remedy Ensign et al. describe: change what gets observed, not the model ([Ensign et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v81/ensign18a.html)). Append-only labels plus frozen old judges re-run on new content separate drift in the world from drift in the model.

### What the evidence qualifies

**The "independent labeller" is less independent than assumed.** Across 349 LLMs, more accurate models make *more* correlated errors, even after controlling for provider and architecture. Sharing a company or a base architecture raises agreement further. One pair agreed on **99.87%** of the questions both got wrong ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07962v1)). If the Jev was distilled from frontier model X, then X, the obvious choice of labeller, is the *least* independent option. Independence has to be measured: record the agreement-when-both-wrong rate on a human-labelled anchor and compare it with chance. Knight and Leveson found the same with software. Twenty-seven programs written independently to one specification, each run on 1,000,000 test cases, failed together far more often than chance (**z = 100.55**). The coincident failures clustered on hard *inputs* ([Knight & Leveson](https://www.kth.se/social/files/564df871f2765419e306178d/KnightLeveson.pdf)).

**Clusters defined by the judge inherit the judge's blind spots.** The best automated slice finder in 2022 recovered only **36%** of planted error slices ([Domino](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14960v2)). Clusters for worst-case reporting should come from content hash, source metadata and an embedding of different lineage, not from the Jev's own features.

**Per-cluster statistics are expensive.** By standard two-proportion arithmetic, detecting a cluster's error rate rising from 5% to 10% at α = 0.05 and 80% power takes about **435 labels per cluster per window**. Detecting a rise from 5% to 7% takes about 2,200. Across 1,000 clusters that is 0.4–2.2 million labels per window, so hierarchical pooling and sequential tests (CUSUM, SPRT) are required rather than optional.

**Determinism cuts both ways.** The fleet is a single fault domain. The Byzantine bound of n ≥ 3f + 1 assumes *independent* faults, and a million copies count as one ([Lamport et al.](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/byz.pdf)). In return, one audit label on a (content, question, judge) key resolves that verdict for every copy. Correlation that is catastrophic for robustness makes auditing cheap.

### Cross-domain precedent says the fatal change is the one nobody staged

In 1970, Texas male-sterile cytoplasm was used to produce about **85%** of U.S. hybrid corn. A new race of southern corn leaf blight exploited a protein specific to that cytoplasm, and some areas lost 100% of their crop ([NAS memoir](https://www.nasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/levings-iii-c-s.pdf)). The follow-up Corn Blight Watch monitored the risk with an independent sensor, remote sensing ([USDA NASS](https://nass.usda.gov/Education_and_Outreach/Reports,_Presentations_and_Conferences/GIS_Reports/Results%20of%20the%201971%20Corn%20Blight%20Watch%20Experiment.pdf)). CrowdStrike's 2024 channel file crashed **over 8.5 million** Windows machines. A template defined 21 input fields, the sensor supplied 20, and the content was pushed to every sensor at once because it was "content," not "code" ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2024/08/07/crowdstrike-outage-causes/)). Knight Capital lost **more than $460 million in 45 minutes** when a deployment reached only part of its routers and reactivated a dormant function ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2013-222)). For the Jev, changes to the rubric, the question text and gate thresholds are *content*, and they need the same canary rings and cluster diffs as weight updates. The log already carries a question hash and a judge hash. It should also carry a hash of the gate configuration, so that partial deployments show up in the record.

### What it suggests building

**A polarization monitor.** Run three or more dissimilar judges on the hash-selected stream. Compare the observed all-wrong and all-agree rates with the Poisson-Binomial independence baseline ([Toups et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.05862v1)). A rising ratio flags a *new* shared blind spot before any labels arrive.

**A churn ledger that can block a ship.** A release fails if any cluster's newly-broken count exceeds a threshold, even when the net change is positive.

**A uniform-random exploration slice.** A small slice of traffic bypasses the Jev's suppression entirely and logs its propensities. Replay evaluation on randomly served data is provably unbiased ([Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.5956)).

**A registry of undeclared consumers.** Every dashboard, ranker and training filter fed by the Jev is listed, so a judge diff reaches every system that inherits it.

**A deliberately weaker, independent judge kept in the loop.** Kleinberg and Raghavan prove that a shared algorithm more accurate for each firm can still *lower* social welfare compared with less accurate independent evaluators ([Kleinberg & Raghavan](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.05853)). Independence can be worth more than the accuracy given up.

## One principle ties it together: nothing scores itself

All four problems fail the same way. The agent that critiques only its own drafts gets worse ([Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01798v2)). The judge that reports its own epistemic uncertainty reports its regularizer ([Jürgens et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.09056v3)). The gate that learns only from outcomes it chose to see becomes optimistic, or, when pointed toward caution, frozen ([Lakkaraju et al.](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/home/kleinber/kdd17-selective.pdf)). The fleet that checks itself with copies of itself gains no diversity ([Diversity prediction theorem](https://RosettaCode.org/wiki/Diversity_prediction_theorem)). The design principle is therefore: **every claim in the system, whether a verdict, a gate decision, a human review, a commitment or a dashboard number, is a forecast, and it is scored by evidence the claimant neither produced nor selected.** Independence is the scarce input. The architecture's job is to mint it cheaply, spend it where value of information is highest, and record its provenance.

Human institutions have already solved most of the mechanics. **Proper scoring rules** (Brier, log) make honest probability reports optimal once ground truth arrives ([Gneiting & Raftery](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)). The Jev should log its full probability vector, not an argmax, so it can be scored. Incentives beat instructions: under the Bayesian Truth Serum, false recognition of nonexistent items was **21%, against 52% under a solemn oath** ([Prelec/Weaver, MIT](https://nel.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/creating-truth-telling.pdf)). Peer prediction can score items that never resolve, but only across *heterogeneous* judges. Same-model copies share both prior and signal and must be pooled as one voter. An "always 0" equilibrium has to be blocked with gold items and scoring that rewards agreement only beyond chance ([Miller et al.](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.1050.0379)). **Risk-limiting audits** commit to public random seeds, accumulate a sequential test, and escalate to a full count if confirmation fails. Denmark's 2014 audit covered about 4.6 million ballots at a 0.1% risk limit with a sample of 1,903 ballots ([Stark](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~stark/Seminars/foro14.pdf)). Comparison audits, which check individual records, need far fewer samples than polling audits ([Stark](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~stark/Seminars/foro14.pdf)). A log keyed by (content, question, judge) is exactly a cast-vote record.

**Double reading with arbitration** is the best-evidenced template for combining small and large judges. It adds **3–11 cancers per 10,000 women screened**. Consensus or arbitration both raises detection *and* lowers recalls, whereas "either reader flags" inflates false positives ([Dinnes et al.](https://www.ceu.ox.ac.uk/publications/191579)). When judges disagree, **debate** lifts weaker judges: non-expert models went from 48% to 76% and humans from 60% to 88% ([Khan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06782)). **Track records** are stable enough to route on. About 70% of superforecasters kept their status the following year, with a year-to-year correlation of **0.65** ([AI Impacts on GJP](https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project/)). Aggregated prediction polls weighted by track record beat prediction markets in IARPA's tournament, which argues for a scoring tournament rather than a tradable market ([Penn Today](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/penn-scientists-show-prediction-polls-can-outdo-prediction-markets)). Extremizing the aggregate helps only across judges with partly independent information, and the size of the gain is contested ([Baron et al.](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/2015---two-reasons-to-make-aggregated-probability-forecasts_1.pdf)). For Jev copies it is actively harmful. **Separation of powers** comes from Sarbanes–Oxley: auditors are hired by an independent committee, lead partners rotate every five years, and the auditors are themselves inspected ([SOX](https://www.congress.gov/bill/107th-congress/house-bill/3763)). The fleet equivalents are distinct keys for producers, auditors and the audit selector; rotation of auditor–producer pairings; and gold items planted in auditors' queues. Rising agreement between auditor and producer with no change in accuracy is a capture signal. Finally, science's own appeals process showed that a verdict rendered once is weak evidence: only **36%** of significant psychology findings replicated as significant ([OSC 2015](https://acawiki.org/Estimating_the_reproducibility_of_psychological_science)). Periodically re-judging a random sample with fresh judges gives each judge a replication rate without needing ground truth.

These institutions converge on one schema change. The judgment log must record **why each entry exists**: the selection mechanism (gate, audit draw, exploration, escalation, human flag) and its probability, plus judge lineage and a hash of the gate configuration. Without propensities, every downstream estimate inherits the selection bias described above. Without lineage, diversity cannot be measured. Without probability vectors, nothing can be scored.

## Conclusion

The fleet's real product is not judgments but **scored** judgments, and its binding constraint is not Jev accuracy but the supply of independent labels. That changes how each feature should be valued. Bit-identical determinism is a robustness liability, but it is also what makes auditing affordable, because one label resolves a verdict for every copy. Shadow judgments, blind audits and hash-selected samples matter less as safety features than as cheap ways to create independent evidence. A blind audit counts three times: as a label, as human practice, and as a routing signal. Seen this way, the "abstention death spiral," human deskilling and baseline drift are one failure: the system stops buying independent evidence because its accounting prices that evidence at zero.

Uncertainty remains high where the fleet is most distinctive. Most quantitative anchors here come from vision benchmarks, forecasting tournaments, medicine and aviation. Nothing was found measuring calibration of ternary or quantized judges, error correlation inside an LLM-judge fleet, or memory ablations in no-execution agents. The first experiments should be the ones only this system can run cheaply:

- per-cluster error correlation between the Jev and candidate labellers;
- Jev calibration before and after teacher recalibration;
- an A/B test of mutable against append-only agent memory;
- the smallest ε and audit floor that keep every region's lower bound moving.
